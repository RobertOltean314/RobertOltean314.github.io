About

I'm a backend developer based in Sibiu. The short version of how I work:

I ask questions until I actually understand. That used to feel like admitting I didn't know something. Now I think it's the cheapest tool I have — five minutes of looking uninformed beats two days building the wrong thing. If a ticket doesn't make sense to me, I'd rather say so than quietly guess at what somebody meant.

I read before I write. Dropped into code I don't know, my instinct isn't to start typing. It's to follow a single request all the way through and find out what shape the system is really in. You can't add to something well until you understand how it already holds together.

I want to know why it works. I'm uncomfortable leaning on an abstraction I couldn't explain, which is most of why my own projects look the way they do. I implemented Groth16 proofs and wrote Huffman, LZ77 and LZW by hand — not because the libraries don't exist, but because importing one would have left me with something I could use and couldn't explain.

What pulls me into a problem is a real constraint. Invoice data can't legally go on a public blockchain — so how do you prove a VAT total without publishing the invoices behind it? A university was paying its staff out of a spreadsheet held together by macros — so what replaces that? I like work where the difficulty is genuine, and where somebody's day is measurably less painful at the end of it.

Selected projects

Bachelor thesis

TaxChain

Prove you paid your VAT — without showing anyone your invoices.

Invoice data can't legally go on a public blockchain. So I implemented Groth16 zero-knowledge proofs (arkworks, BN254) that prove a VAT total for a period without revealing the invoices behind it, with invoice hashes anchored on-chain through a Solidity registry contract for an immutable audit trail. Wrapped in a full accounting workspace: eFactura UBL 2.1 generation, declaration exports, and multi-currency VAT aggregation at live central-bank rates.
